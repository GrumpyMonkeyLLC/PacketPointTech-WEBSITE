That “overdue invoice” email might be a scam

November 2026 · 3 minute read

One of the most common scams aimed at small businesses doesn’t involve a virus at all. It’s an email that looks like it comes from a vendor you already pay, asking you to pay an overdue invoice or telling you their bank details have changed.

If someone pays it, the money goes to the scammer, and it’s usually gone for good.

How it works

Scammers either break into a real vendor’s email account or make one that looks almost the same, such as billing@acme-supply.co instead of billing@acmesupply.com. Then they send something that looks routine:

  • “Your account is past due. Please pay the attached invoice today to avoid a late fee.”
  • “We’ve switched banks. Please send future payments to the account below.”
  • “I’m in a meeting, can you take care of this payment quickly?” (pretending to be your boss)

Because the email can come from a real account and quote real invoice numbers, it can look completely normal.

Warning signs

  • Urgency. “Today,” “immediately,” “before end of business.”
  • New payment details. Any change to where money goes is the biggest red flag of all.
  • A slightly off address. Look at the actual email address, not just the name shown.
  • Asking you to keep it quiet or skip your normal approval steps.

The one habit that stops it: before you pay a new invoice or change anyone’s bank details, call the vendor at a number you already have on file. Never use a phone number from the email itself.

Other things that help

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) for your email, so a stolen password alone isn’t enough to get in.
  • Require two people to approve payments over a set amount.
  • Give your staff a simple rule: anyone can question a payment request, and nobody gets in trouble for checking.

If you think you’ve paid one

Call your bank right away and ask them to recall the payment. The faster you call, the better the odds. Then change the password on any email account that might be involved and report it at ic3.gov.
